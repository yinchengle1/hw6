11Theoretical Background

Independence in Probability

Two categorical random variables are independent when their joint probability equals the product of their marginal probabilities for all categories.

P(X = i, F = j) = P(X = i)P(F = j)

Independence in Statistics

The Chi-Square test compares observed frequencies with the frequencies expected if two variables were independent in the population.

Association vs. Causality

A statistically significant relationship does not prove causation. Other factors, confounding variables, or sampling processes may explain the association.

Test Assumptions

Observations should be independent, categories must be mutually exclusive, and expected frequencies should be sufficiently large for the Chi-Square approximation.
